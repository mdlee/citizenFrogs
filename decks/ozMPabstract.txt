Title: Cognitive Models, Crowd Wisdom, Citizen Science, and Frogs: An Extension of Brown et al (2024)

Author: Michael D. Lee, University of California Irvine

Abstract:

Brown et al (2024) developed a cognitive modeling approach for aggregating people's judgments of the presence of frogs, based on detecting their calls in sound clips taken from field recordings. They used a hierarchical two-high-threshold multinomial processing tree as an account of people's judgments, which separates their detection ability from their guessing biases. They showed that inferences about the presence of frogs using the cognitive modeling approach were more accurate than simple aggregation of the behavioral judgments. We replicate these performance improvements using an even simpler one-high-threshold model without hierarchical individual differences. We then improve the model's performance further by pursuing an idea mentioned but not implemented by Brown et al. Instead of treating the judgments for each sound clip as independent, we implement a simple model of the dynamics of the environment. We assume that frogs can enter of leave the environment over temporally successive clips with some small probability. Adding these environmental dynamics causes inferences about the presence or absence of frogs to share information across clips, and significantly improves the model's performance for many of the frog types.
