Title: Improving a cognitive modeling approach to aggregating people's judgments in ecological monitoring

Author: Michael D. Lee, University of California Irvine

Abstract:

Brown et al (20??) developed a cognitive modeling approach to aggregating people's judgments of the presence of different frogs, based on detecting their calls in sound clips taken from field recordings. Brown et al used a hierarchical two-high threshold multinomial processing tree as an account of people's judgments, which separates their detection ability from their guessing biases. They showed that inferences about the presence of frogs based on the model were more accurate than simple aggregation of the yes-no behavioral judgments. We replicate these performance improvements using an even simpler one-high threshold model without hierarchical individual differences. We then improve the model's performance by pursuing an idea mentioned but not implemented by Brown et al (20??). Instead of treating the judgments for each sound clip as independent, we implement a simple model of the dynamics of the environment. We assume that frogs can enter of leave the environment over temporally successive clips with some small probability. Adding these environmental dynamics causes inferences about the presence or absence of frogs to share information across clips, and significantly improves detection performance for many of the frog types.
